01 — The Thesis

Summary

Three claims, each documented in the sections below.

New England residential water combines non-discretionary demand, state-license-constrained labor supply, and an active consolidation market with entry at ~3–4× owner earnings and platform exits at 8–12× EBITDA. No platform operating today is residential-water-first.

Demand

~1.2M private-well households (~3M residents). No federal regulation applies at a private tap. Arsenic, radon, uranium, iron and PFAS occur naturally or persistently in the bedrock aquifer. Pump failures, failed real-estate tests and MCL exceedances cannot be deferred.

NHDES · Maine CDC · USGS · EPA

Supply

Well-pump work legally requires state licenses (NH: 3 years' experience + exam). NH's licensed pump-installer count fell 22% in the five years to 2025.

NH RSA 482-B · NH Water Well Board minutes, 2025

Exit

159 water-sector transactions closed in 2025. Sub-$5M shops trade at ~3–4× owner earnings; assembled platforms exit at 8–12× EBITDA. The active buyers are named in §04. All are HVAC-led or commercial-led.

Contaminant load

New England sits on fractured granite bedrock. Groundwater moving through it carries:

  • Arsenic — ~1 in 5 NH bedrock wells exceeds the 10 µg/L federal limit (USGS); an estimated 25–30% exceed NH's stricter 5 µg/L state standard, in effect since July 2021. Odorless and tasteless; found only by lab test.
  • Radon — a dissolved radioactive gas; NHDES reports roughly half of NH bedrock wells at levels of concern, against a 2,000 pCi/L advisory level. Off-gasses into household air at every shower.
  • Uranium — same granite, same pathway, its own lab panel and treatment train.
  • Iron & manganese — staining, taste and appliance damage; the most common visible complaint. Manganese: 0.05 mg/L EPA secondary (aesthetic) standard; 0.3 mg/L EPA health advisory.
  • Hardness — common in bedrock wells; scale shortens the life of every water heater and fixture downstream.
  • Low pH — acidic bedrock water corrodes copper plumbing and leaches lead and copper into drinking water.
  • Bacteria — total coliform failures signal surface pathways into the well; automatic deal-breaker on a real-estate test.
  • PFAS — industrial "forever chemicals"; regulation, deadlines and rebates in §03.

No utility monitors these wells. Testing, treatment and repair are initiated by the homeowner and performed by local licensed operators.

NH set enforceable limits in 2020; the 2024 federal rule is stricter. A pending EPA proposal (May 2026) would rescind the federal PFHxS, PFNA, GenX and Hazard Index standards while keeping PFOA/PFOS at 4.0 ppt. EPA's codified best available technologies — granular activated carbon (GAC), anion exchange, reverse osmosis, nanofiltration — are standard residential equipment installed by the operators in §04.

NHDES rebate program. Private wells are not covered by the federal rule; residential PFAS treatment is voluntary. NHDES's PFAS Removal Rebate Program pays homeowners up to $5,000 toward qualifying private-well treatment or $10,000 toward a public-water connection; it is funding-dependent (suspended June 2024, re-funded that September under HB 1649, with homeowners now applying directly to NHDES rather than through a contractor). A whole-house GAC install runs ~$5,200 — the rebate covers ~96% of it. Eligibility is gated on a test result: the well must exceed one of NHDES's four PFAS ambient groundwater quality standards, and the owner must not already have been offered bottled water, treatment or a service connection by a third party. That makes the certified lab test the entry condition for the rebate, not an optional first step. The channel today is held by two sub-$3M marketers (NH Tap, Mainline Plumbing); the larger licensed incumbents do not market it. 06 — How the Systems Work

Residential system architecture

Every private-well home runs the same architecture: extraction, pressurization, then contaminant-by-contaminant treatment ahead of the first tap.

Source

Drilled bedrock well · typically 200–500 ft

Six-inch steel casing through sand and gravel into granite. The submersible pump ($350–$1,600 as a part) hangs on hundreds of feet of drop pipe with a torque arrestor and safety rope. Pulling it is a two-person job with a rig.

Pressure

Pressure tank + switch

An air-bladder tank (20–80 gal) holds pressure so the pump doesn't start on every faucet. Waterlogged tanks and burnt pressure-switch contacts are the most common "no water" causes after the pump itself.

Treatment

The train, in strict order

Sediment filter → calcite pH neutralizer → oxidizing iron/manganese filter → softener + brine tank → UV sterilizer → GAC carbon for PFAS/radon → optional reverse-osmosis at the kitchen tap. Order matters: neutralize before you filter, filter before you soften, disinfect last.

Consumables

What recurs by design

Softener salt (monthly–quarterly), sediment cartridges (quarterly), UV lamps (12-month life), GAC media (2–4 yrs), arsenic media (3–5 yrs), resin rebeds (~5 yrs), annual re-tests.

System architecture: NHDES well-design guidance · vendor installation manuals · AEP analysis.

Before arrival

  1. Dispatch and routeJob lands on the schedule with symptom notes; route sequenced against the other trucks.
  2. Confirmation callTech calls from the previous job with an arrival window.
  3. History pullPrior visits, installed equipment, media age, warranty status. At most shops this lives in paper and memory, not a CRM.

On site

  1. Arrival and walk-upBoot covers on; restate the complaint back to the homeowner in their own words.
  2. Symptom interviewWhen did it start, hot and cold or cold only, all fixtures or one, any recent well work?
  3. Fixture checkRun the nearest cold tap; note pressure, air, color, odor, sediment — the first real data point.

Mechanical diagnosis

  1. Power and controls firstOn any no-water call, electrical leads: breaker, then voltage at the pressure switch and control box — power off before either is opened (Franklin Electric AIM manual).
  2. Pressure tank and switchGauge through a full cycle; listen for short-cycling. Air pre-charge is a separate pump-off, tank-drained check, set 2 psi below cut-in (Amtrol procedure).
  3. Pump and wellheadDraw-down test, amp draw against nameplate, recovery timing; wellhead cap seal, casing height, grade and the electrical connections at the cap (NGWA checklist).

Water quality

  1. Existing treatment auditAge, brand and valve type of every vessel; media condition; bypass position; salt bridge; UV lamp hours; RO membrane date.
  2. Field water testThe fifteen-minute panel (detail below), run on raw water drawn ahead of the treatment train — a post-softener sample corrupts the result — plus sample pull for lab work.
  3. Lab sample handlingArsenic, uranium, radon, coliform and PFAS go to a certified lab under chain-of-custody, with a promised call-back date.

The close

  1. DiagnosisMap results onto the treatment decision tree; sequence the train — neutralize before filtering, filter before softening, UV last.
  2. Show the homeownerPhysical evidence presented: test-strip readings, media condition, casing wear.
  3. Options, priced on the spotGood / better / best from the price book, with the maintenance implications of each, before leaving the basement.
  4. Authorization and schedulingSignature, deposit, install date, and a written note of what was deferred and why.
  5. Maintenance enrollmentSalt, media, lamp and re-test cadence offered as a plan. Rarely performed today; most operators have no program to enroll into.
  6. Documentation and follow-upPhotos, readings and recommendations into the record; a re-test reminder set for twelve months.

How the water gets tested — the lab chain

Anything with a health limit goes out: arsenic, uranium, radon, coliform, metals, and PFAS by EPA Method 537.1 or 533. Samples are pulled on site into lab-supplied bottles, labeled under chain-of-custody, and driven or shipped to state-accredited labs (in NH, NELAC-accredited labs such as Eastern Analytical; competitor Epping Well & Pump owns one outright). Panels run $150–$475 depending on scope; results return in 5–15 business days. At call-back, results are read against limits (e.g., arsenic 12 µg/L vs the NH limit of 5) and a treatment quote follows. Real-estate closings run the same chain inside a 30–45 day contingency window. EPA lab-methods page · Tap Score / accredited-lab price lists · seacoastanalytical.com

09 — The Strategy

Strategy

Four phases; each is gated by the one before it.

Phase 1 · Capacity

Hire before marketing

Build the licensed-tech pipeline first: apprentices on the three-year license clock, WQA certification funded up the ladder, a second install crew.

Phase 2 · Recurring

Productize maintenance

Named membership tiers attached at every install and service call (step 17, §07), pricing indexed to media costs. Converts per-call maintenance billing into contracted recurring revenue.

Phase 3 · Demand

Turn on the ignored channels

Local search on high-rating profiles, PFAS marketing, NHDES rebate administration, and formalized realtor/lender referral channels.

Phase 4 · Consolidate

Buy the retiring incumbents

Tuck-in acquisitions of $1–3M shops at ~3–4× owner earnings as founders exit — each adding licensed technicians, an installed service base, and route density. A-Z Water Systems (ME) executed this model across three acquisitions.

Why residential first: the largest pool (~1.2M well households), fragmented across small independents (§04), referral-driven customer acquisition (§05), and no incumbent platform treats it as the core. Commercial and compliance work are adjacencies sequenced after a residential base.

In summary: ~1.2M unregulated private-well households; state-license-gated labor with a documented 22% five-year decline in NH licensed installers; fixed service schedules on every installed system, currently uncontracted; entry at ~3–4× owner earnings; documented buyers and exits at 8–12× EBITDA.
